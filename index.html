Welcome to my personal page.

I am an independent ML researcher and an MSc student in Computer Science at Abubakar Tafawa Balewa University (ATBU), Bauchi. I am currently a Research Fellow in the Fatima Fellowship, mentored by Karim Farid (University of Freiburg), and an Analyst/Programmer at Child Health and Mortality Prevention Surveillance (CHAMPS) – ATBU. I completed my BSc in Computer Science at Al-Qalam University Katsina in 2023.

My research asks when neural networks genuinely generalize and when they only appear to, both in learning dynamics and in transfer to data-scarce settings, with applications in public health and environmental monitoring. Right now I study an interpolation regime that sits between memorization and generalization during training, and long-range transfer learning for water-quality prediction where local data is scarce.

  1. Preprint
    Between Memorization and Generalization: An Interpolation Regime in Learning Dynamics
    Mahi Aminu Aliyu, Ahmad Imran , and Karim Farid
    2026
    Preprint
    Abs Bib PDF

    Grokking marks the onset of generalization, but not how it unfolds. We study the structure of this transition by separating held-out problems according to whether they can be solved by interpolating from what the model has already seen. In the in-context modular-arithmetic setting of He et al. (2024), who identified two algorithms mechanistically in trained transformers, we follow both through training. Transformers first acquire Ratio Matching, which rescales a single in-context example. Only later, and only with enough context, do they acquire Modular Regression, which combines several examples and reaches beyond interpolation. Shallower models remain confined to Ratio Matching. We observe the same ordering in a controlled family of compositional tasks. Transformers first solve the held-out tasks that are recoverable as least-squares blends of training tasks, and solve the remaining tasks only later. The standard held-out threshold is crossed before this second transition, and MLPs of the same size rarely progress beyond interpolation. The ordering persists under controls for threshold choice and group size and, for MLPs, is not accounted for by task difficulty. More diverse training tasks shorten the interpolation window, whereas harder tasks stretch it. These results suggest that the transition from memorization to generalization is not a single global event, but a sequence of staggered capability transitions that aggregate evaluation collapses into one. We discuss whether this intermediate regime may contribute to plausible-but-incorrect predictions in larger models, including hallucinations.

  3. AfricaNLP
    AfriCaption: Establishing a New Paradigm for Image Captioning in African Languages
    Mardiyyah Oduwole , Prince Mireku , Fatimo Adebanjo , Oluwatosin Olajide , Mahi Aminu Aliyu, and Jekaterina Novikova
    In Proceedings of the 7th Workshop on African Natural Language Processing (AfricaNLP), co-located with EACL 2026 , 2026
    Abs arXiv Bib

    Multimodal AI research has overwhelmingly focused on high-resource languages, hindering the democratization of advancements in the field. To address this, we present AfriCaption, a comprehensive framework for multilingual image captioning in 20 African languages and our contributions are threefold: (i) a curated dataset built on Flickr8k, featuring semantically aligned captions generated via a context-aware selection and translation process; (ii) a dynamic, context-preserving pipeline that ensures ongoing quality through model ensembling and adaptive substitution; and (iii) the AfriCaption model, a 0.5B parameter vision-to-text architecture that integrates SigLIP and NLLB200 for caption generation across under-represented languages. This unified framework ensures ongoing data quality and establishes the first scalable image-captioning resource for under-represented African languages, laying the groundwork for truly inclusive multimodal AI.

  4. arXiv
    When Distributions Shift: Causal Generalization for Low-Resource Languages
    Mahi Aminu Aliyu, Chisom Chibuike , Fatimo Adebanjo , Omokolade Awosanya , and Samuel Oyeneye
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2510.27512, 2025
    Abs arXiv Bib

    Machine learning models often fail under distribution shifts, a problem exacerbated in low-resource settings where limited data restricts robust generalization. Domain generalization (DG) methods address this challenge by learning representations that remain invariant across domains, frequently leveraging causal principles. In this work, we study two causal DG approaches for low-resource natural language processing. First, we apply causal data augmentation using GPT-4o-mini to generate counterfactual paraphrases for sentiment classification on the NaijaSenti Twitter corpus in Yoruba and Igbo. Second, we investigate invariant causal representation learning with the Debiasing in Aspect Review (DINER) framework for aspect-based sentiment analysis. We extend DINER to a multilingual setting by introducing Afri-SemEval, a dataset of 17 languages translated from SemEval-2014 Task. Experiments show improved robustness to unseen domains, with consistent gains from counterfactual augmentation and enhanced out-of-distribution performance from causal representation learning across multiple languages.
